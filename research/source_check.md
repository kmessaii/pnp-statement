# Source check of site/index.html (draft "AI labs should stop working on P = NP")

Checked 2026-11-23 (evening, Pacific), one checker, no helpers. Read-only; nothing under `site/` was touched.

**How the passages were obtained.** Every source link on the page was downloaded with `curl`, and its text was extracted on this laptop (PyMuPDF for PDFs, a plain HTML-to-text pass for web pages). The passages below were copied from that extracted text, not from a summarising fetch tool. Where extraction lost formatting I say so; the usual case is superscripts flattening, so "2^64" comes out as "264". Wiki markup is stripped from the two Wikipedia passages. Anything I reached only through a search engine's summary, or through another reader's file, is marked as such and never given the verdict "matches the original".

**Verdicts:** MATCHES = matches the original. SECONDARY = supported only by secondary coverage. NOT OPENED = could not open the source. WRONG = wrong or overstated.

**Tally:** 70 rows. 11 WRONG, 7 SECONDARY, 2 NOT OPENED, 50 MATCHES. The 50 MATCHES include 17 rows on the source list (rows 54–70), where the check is whether the link resolves and says what the page uses it for.

## Beyond the fact check (my reading, not a sourced finding)

The section "Warning people first is not enough" ends: "Each year it is not found is a year in which more of the world moves to cryptography that can be replaced quickly. That is what stopping buys." The section before it says that under a practical P = NP "There is no patch" and the post-quantum standards "fall with the old ones." A cryptographer will put the two side by side. Being able to swap ciphers quickly protects against a break of one scheme, not against P = NP. Against P = NP the only thing delay buys is that less traffic exists under keys that can be recovered; it buys no better replacement. The argument may need one sentence saying which of the two outcomes the years are for.
